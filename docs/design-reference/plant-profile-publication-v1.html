At home with a Monstera

A tropical climber with real presence

Monstera deliciosa earns its place as an icon. Give it room, steady warmth and something to climb, and each new leaf can become larger and more deeply divided than the last.

In its natural habitat, Monstera climbs towards the canopy using aerial roots. That climbing habit explains most of what the plant wants indoors: filtered light, an open but moisture-retentive compost, and a sturdy support. It is forgiving enough for a first-time grower, but responds visibly to thoughtful care.

Position it near an east- or west-facing window, or set it back from strong south-facing glass. Rotate the pot occasionally for balanced growth, but avoid repeatedly moving a settled plant between very different conditions.

The secret is not more water or more feed—it is giving the plant enough light to use them well.
The essential care

Four decisions that matter

Light

Bright, indirect light encourages larger leaves and stronger fenestration without scorching.

Water

Soak thoroughly, then wait until the upper compost feels dry before watering again.

Humidity

Average UK homes are suitable; stable warmth matters more than occasional misting.

Make another plant

Propagation starts with a node

A leaf without a node may remain attractive in water, but it cannot produce a complete new plant. Choose a healthy cutting with one node and, ideally, an aerial root.

  1. Take a clean cutting just below a viable node.
  2. Root in water or an airy propagation mix in bright, indirect light.
  3. Pot on once several branching roots have formed.

Straight answers

Monstera questions, answered

Practical answers for the situations UK growers encounter most often.

How often should I water a Monstera?

Check the compost rather than following a calendar. Water when the top 3–5 cm feels dry, then let excess water drain fully.

Why are there no splits in the leaves?

Juvenile plants naturally have solid leaves. Mature plants also need strong filtered light and climbing support to produce larger, divided foliage.

Should I mist the leaves?

Misting changes humidity only briefly. Wiping dust from the leaves and avoiding radiators and cold draughts are more useful.

Is Monstera safe around pets?

No. The plant contains calcium oxalate crystals and should be kept away from pets and children likely to chew it.
