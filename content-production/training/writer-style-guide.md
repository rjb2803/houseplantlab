# HouseplantLab writer training — version 1

Status: provisional; trained from one owner-approved tone decision.

## Approved voice

HouseplantLab reads like a premium British gardening publication: calm, observant, assured and immediately useful. It explains uncertainty clearly without sounding hesitant or repetitive.

Approved example:

> A brown mark on a Monstera leaf is easy to notice and surprisingly difficult to diagnose. Watering problems, harsh sunlight, cold draughts and pests can all produce similar damage. The position and texture of the mark provide better clues than its colour alone.

## Reader-facing rules

- Lead with a polished explanation, not a chatty question or an alarmist warning.
- Use specific observations and practical next steps.
- Prefer natural sentence variation over short, repetitive instructions.
- Use British English and metric measurements first.
- Avoid generic SEO filler, exaggerated certainty and repeated caveats.
- Never write visible source IDs, citation markers, Evidence lines, Sources sections or bibliographies.
- Target 1,400-1,800 words of useful detail; never stretch an article with filler or repetition.
- Finish with a 100-220 word section titled exactly `In summary` that synthesises the diagnosis and next actions.
- Keep all evidence traceability privately in the evidence pack and `claimSourceIds` fields.
- Never claim HouseplantLab experience, tests or photography without a dated observation record.
